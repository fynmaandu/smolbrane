Fock Representation of Massive Field Algebra

The vacuum state on the extended asymptotic Klein-Gordon algebra \(\mathscr{A}^{\text{KG}}_{\text{in,QP}}\) is the Gaussian algebraic state \(\omega^{\text{KG}}_0\) with vanishing \(1\)-point function and \(2\)-point function given by \[\begin{equation} \label{Vacuums_KG} \omega^{\text{KG}}_0\big( \boldsymbol{b}(w_1)\boldsymbol{b}(w_2)^* \big) = \frac{4(2\pi)^3}{m^2} \langle w_1, w_2\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\hspace{1em} \omega^{\text{KG}}_0\big( c(w_1)c(w_2)^* \big) = \frac{4(2\pi)^3}{m^2} \langle w_2, w_1\rangle_{\mathcal{H}} \end{equation}\] for all test functions \(w_1(p)\), \(w_2(p)\), where \(\langle,\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\) is the \(L^2\) inner product on \(\mathcal{H}^-\). Furthermore, \(\omega^{\text{KG}}_0\) is an eigenstate of eigenvalue zero of \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Q}}_{i^-}(\lambda)\) and all of the Poincaré generators.

GNS Construction (Massive Scalar Field)

The GNS construction for \(\omega_0^{\mathrm{KG}}\) yields a Hilbert space \(\mathscr{F}^{\mathrm{KG}}\) with a natural Fock space structure.

  • Concretely this construction is obtained as follows.

  • On the space of complex test functions on \(\mathcal{H}^-\) we define the inner product

\[\begin{equation} \langle w_1 | w_2 \rangle := \omega_0^{\mathrm{KG}}\big(\mathbf{b}(w_1)\mathbf{b}(w_2)^*\big) = \frac{4(2\pi)^3}{m^2} \langle w_1, w_2 \rangle_{\mathcal{H}} \end{equation}\]

using the 2-point function in [Vacuums_KG] .

  • This is a \(\underbrace{\text{non-degenerate}}\), positive, Hermitian inner product

  • This Hilbert space serves as the “one particle” Hilbert space for particles in the Fock space.

The Fock space of particles is given by

\[\begin{equation} \mathscr{F}^{\mathrm{KG}}_{\mathrm{particles}} = \mathbb{C} \oplus \bigoplus_{n \geq 1} \underbrace{ \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KG}}_S \otimes \cdots \otimes_S \mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{KG}} }_{n~\text{times}} \end{equation}\]

where \(\otimes_S\) is the symmetrized tensor product. On this Fock space the \(\mathbf{b}(w)^*\) acts as a creation operator and \(\mathbf{b}(w)\) acts as the annihilation operator for a particle wave packet \(w(p)\).
